Wiring the robot

Connecting it all up: the battery, the ESCs, the motors and the ESP32 — and which GPIO pin ends up driving which wheel.

The written guide isn't finished yet. The diagram below is the team's Rev A drawing, redrawn from the whiteboard sketch. Its signal pin order (red / black / blue) is as sketched: confirm it against the SPARK signal header before you crimp anything, and ask a mentor if your parts don't match. Programming the robot explains how ports are named in code once yours are wired.

Wiring diagram

Two 7.6 V LiPo packs in parallel feed the main breaker, then the power distribution board: a 5 A slot for the MiniCore and a 20 A slot for each SPARK motor controller. Port 17 signals SPARK A (motor A), port 16 signals SPARK B (motor B), and ports 18 and 19 stay open.

The MiniCore board in 3D

The ESP32 controller everything above connects to. Turn it to find the power input, the antenna connector and the signal header ports before you plug anything in.

What this page will cover

The outline below is what the finished guide will walk through.

  • How power flows Battery to ESCs to motors, and where the ESP32 gets its supply — the map to have in your head before you connect anything.
  • Battery and connectors Which connector goes where, polarity, and the switch.
  • ESCs to motors The three-wire motor connections, and what to do when a wheel turns the wrong way.
  • ESC signal wires to the ESP32 Which GPIO pins carry the signal, and how they line up with the left_motor_pin and right_motor_pin settings in your code.
  • Grounds Where they tie together, and why a missing one gives you a robot that twitches for no visible reason.
  • The optional OLED Its two I²C wires, and how to turn it off in code if you aren't fitting one.
  • Check before first power-on What to inspect and measure before the battery goes in — the step that catches the mistakes that let smoke out.
  • When it browns out Bulk capacitance across the ESC supply and battery connector checks. The library already ramps the motors to bound current draw, but that cannot fix wiring that couldn't supply it.
